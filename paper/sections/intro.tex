\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In the five cancer-predisposition genes studied here, 81\% of the 11,010 ClinVar missense records classified as (likely)
pathogenic, (likely) benign or of uncertain significance are variants of uncertain significance (VUS; our count from the
release of 26 October 2026 \cite{Landrum2018}), and for many of them computational predictors are among the few sources
of evidence \cite{Pejaver2022}. The ACMG/AMP guidelines of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the
Association for Molecular Pathology grade evidence qualitatively \cite{Richards2015}; their Bayesian adaptation equates
each strength with a likelihood ratio, the odds of pathogenicity (OddsPath) \cite{Tavtigian2018,Brnich2020}.
Calibration workflows report this ratio as a point estimate or a resampling-based bound
\cite{Pejaver2022,Bergquist2025,Badonyi2025}, but for a single gene few labelled variants are available and the
threshold is chosen from the same data; these bounds are not designed to remain valid after that choice. Two
features of the data make the estimate fragile. The share of damaging variants differs between calibration sets and the
variants being classified: 45\% of confidently labelled ClinVar missense variants in our genes are pathogenic, whereas
6.0\% to 25.9\% of the other assayed variants lose function in saturation genome editing (SGE) or deep mutational
scanning (DMS) experiments (\cref{tab:data}). And labelled sets are small, with dependent substitutions at each residue:
when the population likelihood ratio was known, thresholds selected on percentile-bootstrap bounds respected every
claimed bound in only 57.6\% of resampled data sets (\cref{sec:evidence}).

We introduce \textsc{CertiVar} (\cref{fig:overview}), which turns a predictor score into loss-of-function (LoF),
functional or no calls by two cut-offs: per-class (Mondrian) conformal thresholds, set from SGE or DMS labels with one
variant per residue, bound how often each class is mis-called, and a certification step bounds the likelihood ratio of
each call type with respect to the assay outcome after a threshold is selected from a grid. Neither step needs the LoF
share of the variants being called, provided their within-class score distribution matches that of the assayed
variants, and a mixture-consistency check tests a necessary condition for this. The certified quantity is evidence about
pathogenicity only to the extent that the assay is clinically validated. Our contributions are: (1) certified likelihood
ratios for both call types of an abstaining classifier on residue-clustered data, valid after threshold selection with
a Bonferroni correction (evidence levels also with fixed-sequence testing), with a coverage guarantee after selection and disjoint call sets
(\cref{sec:method}); (2) a label-free check of class-conditional invariance, which kept close to its 5\% level on random
splits and rejected every tested set of unassayed VUS outside the assayed residues (\cref{sec:check}); (3) a benchmark
against known population likelihood ratios in which exact and Katz bounds held while bootstrap bounds and point
estimates overstated evidence (\cref{sec:evidence}); and (4) evidence on five genes and three predictors that marginal and
prior-reweighted conformal prediction lose coverage of one class under a shift of the calibration LoF share, while
per-class coverage holds (\cref{sec:coverage}).
