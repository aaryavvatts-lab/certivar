\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In the five cancer-predisposition genes studied here, 81\% of the ClinVar missense records classified as (likely)
pathogenic, (likely) benign or uncertain are variants of uncertain significance (VUS; our count from the 26 October 2026 release
\cite{Landrum2018}), and for many of
them computational predictors are among the few sources of evidence \cite{Pejaver2022}. The guidelines of the American
College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) grade evidence
qualitatively \cite{Richards2015}; their Bayesian adaptation equates each strength with a likelihood ratio, the odds of
pathogenicity (OddsPath) \cite{Tavtigian2018,Brnich2020}. Calibration workflows report this ratio as a point estimate or
a resampling-based bound \cite{Pejaver2022,Bergquist2025,Badonyi2025}. For a single gene, few labelled variants are
available and the threshold that defines the evidence is chosen from the same data; these bounds are not designed to remain valid
after this choice.

Two features of the data make the estimate fragile. First, the share of damaging variants differs between calibration
sets and the variants being classified, and is unknown for the latter: in our genes, 45\% of confidently labelled
ClinVar missense variants are pathogenic, whereas 6.0\% to 25.9\% of the other assayed missense variants lose function
in saturation genome editing (SGE) or deep mutational scanning (DMS) experiments (\cref{tab:data}). Second, gene-level
labelled sets are small and substitutions at one residue are dependent: in a resampling design with known truth,
percentile-bootstrap bounds were respected in only 76.5\% of threshold selections (\cref{sec:evidence}).

We introduce \textsc{CertiVar} (\cref{fig:overview}), which turns a predictor score into loss-of-function (LoF),
functional or no calls.
Per-class (Mondrian) conformal thresholds set from SGE or DMS labels bound how often each class is mis-called, and a
certification step bounds the likelihood ratio of each call type with respect to the assay outcome, valid after a
threshold has been selected; neither needs an estimate of the LoF share. The certified quantity is how strongly a call
predicts the assay result; it is evidence about pathogenicity only to the extent that the assay is clinically validated.
Our contributions: (1) a formulation of predictor-based calling as abstaining classification on residue-clustered data,
with proofs that the certified likelihood-ratio bounds hold after threshold selection, that per-class coverage after
selection is at least one minus the largest grid level, and that a fixed-sequence variant needs no multiplicity correction over the grid for one evidence level (\cref{sec:method}); (2) a known-truth benchmark in which exact binomial bounds held where
bootstrap bounds and point estimates did not (\cref{sec:evidence}); (3) evidence on five genes and three predictors that
per-class coverage stays at or near the target when the calibration LoF share moves from 5\% to 50\%, where marginal and
prior-reweighted conformal prediction fail (\cref{sec:coverage}); and (4) where the guarantees stop (\cref{sec:transfer}).
