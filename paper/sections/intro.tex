\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In the five cancer-predisposition genes studied here, 81\% of the 11,010 ClinVar missense records classified as (likely)
pathogenic, (likely) benign or of uncertain significance are variants of uncertain significance (VUS; our count from the
release of 26 October 2026 \cite{Landrum2018}), and for many of them computational predictors are among the few sources
of evidence \cite{Pejaver2022}. The guidelines of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the
Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) grade evidence qualitatively \cite{Richards2015}; their Bayesian
adaptation equates each strength with a likelihood ratio, the odds of pathogenicity (OddsPath)
\cite{Tavtigian2018,Brnich2020}. Calibration workflows report this ratio as a point estimate or a resampling-based bound
\cite{Pejaver2022,Bergquist2025,Badonyi2025}. For a single gene, few labelled variants are available and the threshold
that defines the evidence is chosen from the same data; these bounds are not designed to remain valid after that
choice.

Two features of the data make the estimate fragile. First, the share of damaging variants differs between calibration
sets and the variants being classified, and is unknown for the latter: in our genes, 45\% of confidently labelled
ClinVar missense variants are pathogenic, whereas 6.0\% to 25.9\% of the other assayed missense variants lose function
in saturation genome editing (SGE) or deep mutational scanning (DMS) experiments (\cref{tab:data}). Second, gene-level
labelled sets are small and substitutions at one residue are dependent: when the population likelihood ratio was known,
thresholds selected on percentile-bootstrap bounds respected every claimed bound in only 57.6\% of resampled data sets
(\cref{sec:evidence}).

We introduce \textsc{CertiVar} (\cref{fig:overview}), which turns a predictor score into loss-of-function (LoF),
functional or no calls by two cut-offs. Per-class (Mondrian) conformal thresholds, set from SGE or DMS labels with one
variant per residue, bound how often each class is mis-called. A certification step bounds the likelihood ratio of each
call type with respect to the assay outcome, valid after a threshold has been selected from a grid. Neither step needs
the LoF share of the variants being called, provided their within-class score distribution matches that of the assayed
variants; a mixture-consistency check tests a necessary condition for this on the unlabelled targets. The certified
quantity is how strongly a call predicts the assay result; it is evidence about pathogenicity only to the extent that
the assay is clinically validated. Our contributions are: (1) certified likelihood ratios for the two call types of an
abstaining classifier on residue-clustered data, valid after threshold selection with a Bonferroni correction or with
fixed-sequence testing, per level or simultaneously over levels, together with a coverage guarantee after selection and
disjoint call sets (\cref{sec:method}); (2) a mixture-consistency check of class-conditional invariance that needs no
target labels, kept its 5\% level on random splits and rejected every tested set of unassayed VUS outside the assayed
residues (\cref{sec:check}); (3) a benchmark against known population likelihood ratios in which exact and Katz bounds
held while percentile-bootstrap bounds and point estimates overstated evidence (\cref{sec:evidence}); and (4) evidence
on five genes and three predictors that marginal and prior-reweighted conformal prediction lose coverage of one class
when the calibration LoF share moves from 5\% to 50\%, while per-class coverage holds as the theory predicts
(\cref{sec:coverage}).
