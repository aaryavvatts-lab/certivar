\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
Assumptions, propositions, theorems and the corollary are stated in \cref{sec:theory}; $Q$, $C$, $\hat t_k$, the call
rates $\lambda_k,\phi_k$ and their bounds, $L^+$ and $U^-$ are defined in \cref{sec:method}.


Throughout, fix a class $k$ and write $F_k$ for the distribution function of the nonconformity $r_k$ of a variant drawn
from $Q(\cdot\mid Y=k)$. By Assumption 1, the kept calibration variants (one per residue of $\mathcal A$)
and the variant to be called are independent draws from $Q$; conditionally on all labels, the $n_k$ kept variants of
class $k$ and a class-$k$ test variant are independent draws from $Q(\cdot\mid Y=k)$.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.}
Conditionally on the labels, the $n_k+1$ nonconformities of the class-$k$ kept variants and of the test variant are
exchangeable, so with ties broken at random the rank of the test value is uniform on $\{1,\dots,n_k+1\}$
\cite{Vovk2005,Angelopoulos2023}. The test value is at most the $j_k$-th smallest calibration value with probability
$j_k/(n_k+1)$, which lies in $[1-\alpha_k,\,1-\alpha_k+1/(n_k+1))$ for $j_k=\lceil (n_k+1)(1-\alpha_k)\rceil$; ties only
increase the probability, which gives the lower bound in general. If $j_k>n_k$ then $\hat t_k=\infty$. Since $k\in C$
exactly when $r_k\le\hat t_k$, the claim follows. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proposition 2.}
Conditionally on the labels, the coverage of the threshold is $F_k(\hat t_k)=F_k(r^{(k)}_{(j)})$. If $F_k$ is continuous,
$F_k(r^{(k)}_{(j)})$ is the $j$-th order statistic of $n_k$ independent uniforms and has the
$\mathrm{Beta}(j,n_k-j+1)$ distribution, so $\Pr\{F_k(\hat t_k)<1-\alpha_k\}=F_{\mathrm{Beta}(j,n_k-j+1)}(1-\alpha_k)\le\delta_{\mathrm P}$
by the choice of $j_k$ \cite{Vovk2013}. If $F_k$ has atoms, write $r=F_k^{-1}(U)$ with $U$ uniform and
$F_k^{-1}$ the generalized inverse; then $r^{(k)}_{(j)}=F_k^{-1}(U_{(j)})$ and $F_k(F_k^{-1}(u))\ge u$, so
$F_k(r^{(k)}_{(j)})\ge U_{(j)}$ and the bound still holds. If no $j\le n_k$ qualifies, $\hat t_k=\infty$ and coverage
is 1. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proposition 3.}
Fix $\mathcal A$. Both ranks in \eqref{eq:rank} are non-increasing in $\alpha$ (the in-expectation rank because
$\lceil (n_k+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$ is, the PAC rank because $F_{\mathrm{Beta}(j,n_k-j+1)}(1-\alpha)$ decreases in $\alpha$
for each $j$, so the smallest qualifying $j$ cannot increase), so $\hat t_k(\alpha)$ is non-increasing in $\alpha$ and the set $\{r_k\le\hat t_k(\alpha)\}$ shrinks as
$\alpha$ grows. Hence, for any $\hat\alpha\in G$ that depends on $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ but not on the test variant,
$\Pr(k\in C_{\hat\alpha}\mid Y=k,\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\ge\Pr(k\in C_{\max G}\mid Y=k,\mathcal A)$, and taking
expectations over $\mathcal A$ and Proposition~1 at $\alpha_k=\max G$ give the first claim. For the PAC rank at level
$\delta_{\mathrm P}/|G|$, Proposition~2 applied to each $\alpha\in G$ and a union bound give, with probability at least
$1-\delta_{\mathrm P}$,
coverage at least $1-\alpha$ for every $\alpha\in G$ simultaneously, in particular for $\hat\alpha$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Theorem 1.}
The thresholds $\hat t(\alpha)$ depend only on $\mathcal A$ and are fixed given $\mathcal A$. Conditionally on
$\mathcal A$ and on the labels of the kept variants of $\mathcal B$, the class-1 kept variants of $\mathcal B$ are
$n_1$ independent draws from $Q(\cdot\mid Y=1)$, so the number of them called LoF is
$\mathrm{Binomial}(n_1,\lambda_1(\alpha))$ with $\lambda_1(\alpha)=\Pr(\text{call LoF}\mid Y=1)$, and likewise for the
other three counts. Each one-sided Clopper--Pearson bound \cite{ClopperPearson1934} at level $\delta/(2|G|)$ fails with
probability at most $\delta/(2|G|)$; the conventions for $x=0$, $x=n$ and $n=0$ in Section 3 give the trivial
bounds 0 and 1, which never fail. For $\mathrm{LR}^+$ there are $2|G|$ such bounds ($\underline{\lambda}_1$ and
$\overline{\lambda}_0$ for each $\alpha$), so by the union bound all of them hold simultaneously with probability at
least $1-\delta$. On that event $\lambda_1(\alpha)\ge\underline{\lambda}_1(\alpha)$ and
$\lambda_0(\alpha)\le\overline{\lambda}_0(\alpha)$ for every $\alpha\in G$, hence
$\mathrm{LR}^+(\hat t(\alpha))\ge L^+(\alpha)$ for every $\alpha$ (with $\mathrm{LR}^+=\infty$ when
$\lambda_0(\alpha)=0$), including any $\hat\alpha^+$ chosen from these data. Since this probability bound holds for
every $\mathcal A$, it also holds unconditionally. The argument for $\mathrm{LR}^-$ is identical with $\overline{\phi}_1$
and $\underline{\phi}_0$.
The event does not involve the levels $\tau$, so it covers every evidence level reported. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Theorem 2.}
Fix $\mathcal A$, so the thresholds and the hypotheses $H_\alpha:\mathrm{LR}^+(\hat t(\alpha))\le\tau$ are fixed. (a)
If $H_\alpha$ is true and both endpoints $\underline{\lambda}_1(\alpha)$ and $\overline{\lambda}_0(\alpha)$ at level
$\delta'/2$ hold, then $\underline{\lambda}_1\le\lambda_1\le\tau\lambda_0\le\tau\overline{\lambda}_0$, so $H_\alpha$ is
not rejected; hence a true $H_\alpha$ is rejected with probability at most $\delta'$. Testing stops at the first
non-rejection, so the rejected set is a prefix of the order. Let $\alpha^*$ be the first $\alpha$ in the order for which
$H_\alpha$ is true (if there is none, every claim is true). The procedure claims the level at an $\alpha$ where
$H_\alpha$ is true only if it rejects every hypothesis up to that $\alpha$, which includes $H_{\alpha^*}$; this has
probability at most $\delta'$. The selected $\alpha$ is one of the rejected ones, so the claimed level holds with
probability at least $1-\delta'$. (b) With $\delta'=\delta/L$ for each of $L$ levels, a union bound over the levels shows
that some claimed level fails with probability at most $\delta$. The functional case is identical with
$\overline{\phi}_1/\underline{\phi}_0<\tau'$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Corollary 1.}
Every quantity bounded above is a probability conditional on the true class: $\Pr(k\in C\mid Y=k)$, the four call
rates $\lambda_1,\lambda_0,\phi_1,\phi_0$, and their ratios. Under Assumption 2 these are the same for target variants as for a test variant under
Assumption 1, whatever $\pi$ is, so Propositions 1--3 and Theorems 1--2 transfer. The two call sets are
disjoint for any $\alpha^+,\alpha^-\in G$: a LoF call at $\alpha^+$ requires $q>\hat t_0(\alpha^+)$ and
$1-q\le\hat t_1(\alpha^+)$, a functional call at $\alpha^-$ requires $q\le\hat t_0(\alpha^-)$ and
$1-q>\hat t_1(\alpha^-)$, and both thresholds are non-increasing in $\alpha$, so the first conditions contradict
each other if $\alpha^-\ge\alpha^+$ and the second if $\alpha^-<\alpha^+$. A functional call on a LoF variant requires $1\notin C$, so its rate is at most $1-\Pr(1\in C\mid Y=1)
\le\alpha_1$. None of these quantities involves $\pi$, so no prior is needed to certify them; this concerns the
likelihood ratio with respect to the assay outcome, not evidence about pathogenicity. \hfill$\square$

\section{Simulation}\label{s:sim}
 Scores were drawn per residue as $s=2y+u_c+e$, with a residue effect $u_c\sim N(0,0.5^2)$ shared by
the five variants of a residue, unit Gaussian noise, and the class shared within a residue. A Platt calibrator was fitted
on 600 variants at LoF share 0.45, conformal thresholds were set on another 600 (one variant per residue), and 3,000 test
variants were drawn at LoF shares from 0.05 to 0.60, with 300 replicates per share (\cref{fig:sim}). Per-class
thresholds kept LoF coverage at the target at every share; pooled thresholds did not, and reweighting them \cite{Podkopaev2021} to the EM
estimate of the share \cite{Saerens2002} kept marginal coverage near the target while LoF coverage fell further as LoF variants
became rarer. Adjusting posteriors to the EM estimate removed the calibration error caused by the shift in this
simulation, where the shift is a pure change of prior, unlike in the real data (\cref{sec:coverage}).

\paragraph{Why marginal thresholds do not control the minority class.}
A pooled split-conformal threshold $\hat t$ is a quantile of the mixture $\pi_c F_1+(1-\pi_c)F_0$ of the two
class-conditional nonconformity laws, weighted by the calibration share $\pi_c$. Its coverage of class 1 is
$F_1(\hat t)$, which is not controlled; when LoF variants are rare and harder to separate, $F_1(\hat t)<1-\alpha$ while
marginal coverage is still near $1-\alpha$. Reweighting to a known test prior \cite{Podkopaev2021} restores marginal
coverage at that prior but does not control $F_1(\hat t)$ either.

\section{Implementation}\label{s:impl}
Inputs: ClinVar \nolinkurl{variant_summary.txt.gz} of 26 October 2026 (SHA-256 in \nolinkurl{data/MANIFEST.txt});
MaveDB score sets \nolinkurl{urn:mavedb:00000097-0-2} (BRCA1), \nolinkurl{00001279-a-1} (BRCA2), \nolinkurl{00000662-0-1}
(BAP1), \mbox{\nolinkurl{00000673-0-1}} (RAD51C) and \nolinkurl{00000050-a-1} (MSH2, positive scores = loss of function); Ensembl VEP
REST on GRCh38 (release 116) with the AlphaMissense and REVEL annotations; ESM-2 \nolinkurl{esm2_t33_650M_UR50D}.
Distance from the class boundary is the absolute difference between a variant's assay score
and the midpoint of the two class medians in its gene, divided by the distance between the medians. Expected calibration
error uses ten equal-mass bins. AUROC intervals are percentiles of 1,000 bootstrap resamples of residues. The region split
divides each gene's assayed residues into four contiguous blocks with equal numbers of distinct positions. ClinVar records
whose aggregate germline classification is empty (somatic-only submissions) are treated as unclassified. All random
draws use NumPy generators with fixed seeds (2027 plus a fixed offset per experiment). The experiments run on a laptop CPU with Python 3.13, NumPy 2.5,
SciPy 1.18, scikit-learn 1.9 and pandas 3.1; ESM-2 scoring used fair-esm 2.0 and PyTorch 2.14 on an Apple-silicon GPU.
Exact versions and data checksums are in the repository. The Platt map is checked to be increasing (positive
slope) every time it is fitted, and every fit in the experiments passed. Certified bounds use the conventions of
\cref{sec:method}: $U^-=\infty$ when $\underline{\phi}_0=0$, an empirical ratio $x/0$ is $\infty$ for $x>0$, and $0/0$ is
undefined (never a claim); boundary cases are covered by unit tests (\texttt{make test}). The VUS-shift LoF shares
are residue-weighted for both the calibration sample (one variant per residue) and the VUS (each residue weighted
equally, then its variants).

\paragraph{Comparison rules (\cref{tab:oracle}).} All rules select, for each level, the grid point with the largest yield
whose bound meets the level (functional calls among $\alpha$ at least the LoF choice of the same strength). Uncorrected
Clopper--Pearson: endpoints at level $\delta/2$ for every grid point. Katz: the one-sided log interval for a ratio of two
binomial proportions \cite{Katz1978} at level $\delta/|G|$ per ratio, with 0.5 added to every cell of a ratio that has a
zero count. Percentile bootstrap: 2,000 multinomial resamples of the certification residues, the 5th percentile of
$\mathrm{LR}^+$ and the 95th of $\mathrm{LR}^-$; corrected: the $100\,\delta/(2|G|)$ percentiles; $+0.5$ counts: 0.5 added to
each of the six cells of every resample. Fixed sequence: levels tested as in \cref{sec:method}, at $\delta/L$ with $L=3$
(supporting, moderate, strong) for the simultaneous version and at $\delta$ for the per-level version.

\paragraph{Mixture check.} The statistic of \cref{sec:method} is evaluated on the union of the observed scores, with
$\pi$ on 201 equally spaced values in $[0,1]$. Each of the 300 bootstrap resamples redraws the two calibration classes
with replacement at their observed sizes and a target sample of the observed size from the fitted mixture of the
empirical class laws; the $p$-value is $(1+\#\{D^*\ge D\})/301$. In a simulation with Gaussian classes the check rejected
4\% of 100 target sets that satisfied invariance and 96\% of 50 that did not. Calibration (one variant per residue) and
target (one per residue) are drawn afresh in every repetition; \cref{tab:designs} gives the designs.

\section{Coverage and certification figures, and further results}\label{s:figs}
\Cref{fig:main} shows per-class coverage by method, per split and on VUS (\cref{sec:coverage,sec:shift});
\cref{fig:shift} shows coverage under a shift of the calibration LoF share and the certification yields of the
Bonferroni and fixed-sequence rules (\cref{sec:coverage,sec:evidence}).

\paragraph{Further results.} In MSH2, with 6\% LoF variants, pooled conformal prediction left the functional class
marginally below 0.90 (\cref{tab:main}), so which class a marginal threshold under-covers depends on the gene. Calibrating
on all variants of the calibration half instead of one per residue used about five times as many labels and abstained
less (30.4\% against 35.5\%); but in a separate run of 50 halvings that compared the two, worst-class coverage fell below
0.90 in 12 of 15 pairs (lowest 0.885), against 5 (lowest 0.897) for one variant per residue. All-variant calibration
weights variants while the test weights residues; weighting each residue equally left 1 pair below 0.90 (lowest 0.898)
but abstained more (44.6\%), which points to the weighting rather than the dependence, so we keep one variant per
residue. Under the calibration-share shift, a separate run balanced at 50\% LoF gave worst-class coverage of
0.891--0.925. In the oracle design, claimed levels held in 99.9\% of certified claims when the likelihood ratio was
weighted by variant rather than by residue; testing each level by fixed sequence at the full $\delta$, which guarantees
one level at a time, reached LoF-moderate in 30.5\% of draws on $G_4$; and functional levels tested in increasing
$\alpha$ reached functional-supporting in 24.7\% of draws against 61.9\% in decreasing order. The control label rule
left 76.8\% of the authors' functional RAD51C variants intermediate, which is why $n$ falls from 2,225 to 832 in
\cref{tab:app_labels} (\texttt{label\_rule\_comparison} in \texttt{paper\_numbers.json}). With 150 calibration
residues drawn at random, coverage averaged over genes met the target with 40.5\% abstention (worst pair 0.890; 82.8\%
abstention with 25 residues; \cref{fig:practical}A). Coverage was lower on the third of variants nearest the assay class
boundary (0.865 LoF, 0.873 functional; \cref{s:strata}). Against ClinVar labels (exploratory, not independent of the
assays), LoF calls had likelihood-ratio lower bounds of 4.9--19.6 (BRCA1), 7.6--9.4 (BRCA2) and 2.6--3.2 (MSH2)
(\cref{tab:app_clinvar}).
