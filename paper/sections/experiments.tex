\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data and protocol}\label{sec:data}
\input{tables/tab_data}
We took every GRCh38 missense single-nucleotide variant in BRCA1, BRCA2, BAP1, RAD51C and MSH2 that is in ClinVar
\cite{Landrum2018} (release of 26 October 2026) or was assayed, annotated each on its MANE Select transcript with the
Ensembl Variant Effect Predictor \cite{McLaren2016} (AlphaMissense \cite{Cheng2023}, REVEL \cite{Ioannidis2016}) and
computed ESM-2 650M \cite{Lin2023} log-likelihood ratios by wild-type marginals \cite{Meier2021}. Labels come from five
MaveDB \cite{Esposito2019} score sets (\cref{tab:data}; accessions in Supplement~S5): the authors' classes for BRCA1,
BAP1 and RAD51C, the authors' threshold for MSH2 (positive loss-of-function score \cite{Jia2021}), and, for BRCA2, which
deposits no classes, Gaussians fitted to the assay's synonymous and nonsense variants (LoF if the posterior is at least
0.95, functional if at most 0.05). Because this control rule disagreed with the authors' classes for 216 of the 4,023 BAP1 variants
confident under both, we repeat the main experiments under alternative label rules (\cref{sec:transfer}). We excluded the 729
assayed variants in splice regions and set aside the 1,274 intermediate variants. One Platt map per predictor, pooled
over genes, used the 859 ClinVar missense variants with at least two review stars and a P/LP or B/LB call (45.1\%
P/LP), which are excluded from every assay-based evaluation set, leaving 14,477 assayed variants with a binary label. Pooled over
genes, AlphaMissense has an area under the ROC curve (AUROC) of 0.946 on the ClinVar set, 0.840 on the other assayed
variants and 0.779 on assayed VUS (non-overlapping 95\% residue-bootstrap intervals). Baselines are pooled (marginal)
split conformal prediction; label-shift-weighted conformal prediction \cite{Podkopaev2021} with the true target share or
its expectation--maximization (EM) estimate \cite{Saerens2002}; fixed tiers ($q\ge0.9$ LoF, $q\le0.1$ functional); the
published AlphaMissense classes ($>0.564$, $<0.34$); and the REVEL PP3/BP4 thresholds ($\ge0.644$, $\le0.290$)
\cite{Pejaver2022}. None claims per-class coverage, and the published cut-offs target pathogenicity, so we judge them
only as assay classifiers. Coverage is residue-weighted as in $Q$; \emph{worst-class coverage} is the smaller of the mean
LoF and mean functional coverages over repetitions. Every split is by residue (designs in Supplement~S6).

\subsection{Per-class coverage, and a shift in the calibration LoF share}\label{sec:coverage}
Over 50 random halvings of each gene's residues (calibrate on one half, test on the other), the in-expectation rule at
$\alpha=0.1$ gave worst-class coverage between 0.896 and 0.908 in all 15 gene--predictor pairs, none more than one Monte
Carlo standard error (0.002--0.010) below the target (\cref{fig:main}A; per-pair values in Table~S1). Pooled conformal
fell to 0.545 on LoF variants and below 0.85 in 5 pairs, because a marginal threshold controls coverage averaged over the two classes, not within each; the published AlphaMissense and REVEL cut-offs covered as little as 0.540 (BAP1) and 0.445 (MSH2) of functional variants. The per-class rule abstained on 35\% of variants on average (17--47\%), against 42\% for pooled conformal
and 11\% for the AlphaMissense classes, which trade coverage for decisiveness. Calibrating on all variants instead of
one per residue used five times as many labels and abstained less (30.4\% against 35.2\%), but worst-class coverage fell
below 0.90 in 13 of 15 pairs (4 of 15 with one variant per residue).

To test whether per-class thresholds need a prevalence estimate, we subsampled each calibration half to a LoF share of
0.05 to 0.5 and tested at the natural share (100 repetitions; \cref{fig:shift}A). The per-class rule kept worst-class
coverage between 0.897 and 0.925 in every pair at every share (0.891--0.925 in a separate run balanced at 50\% LoF),
with abstention rising from 37\% to 83\% as LoF variants became scarce. Pooled conformal calibrated at share 0.05
covered LoF variants at 0.113 in its worst pair, and label-shift-weighted conformal fell to 0.53--0.60 with the true
target share and to 0.04 with the EM estimate, which missed the LoF share by 0.255 on average because ClinVar
pathogenicity and assay loss of function differ in definition as well as in class share.

\subsection{Reliability across splits and a real change of population}\label{sec:shift}
With 17 to 93 LoF residues per calibration half, a single split can miss the in-expectation guarantee: over 200 splits
per pair, LoF coverage was below 0.90 in 36.4\% of splits and functional coverage in 43.6\% (\cref{fig:main}B). PAC
thresholds ($\delta=0.1$) reduced these to 7.9\% and 10.8\% (test-half estimates of the coverage that Proposition~\ref{prop:pac} bounds) at the price of abstaining on 58.4\% of variants against
35.4\%. Calibrated on non-VUS variants of one half of each gene's residues and applied to the assayed VUS of the other
half (residue-weighted LoF share 17.0\% in calibration, 19.8\% in VUS), in-expectation thresholds kept LoF coverage between 0.854 and 0.958
and functional coverage between 0.841 and 0.942, each below 0.90 in 7 of 15 pairs (\cref{fig:main}C); PAC thresholds
stayed at or above 0.957 (LoF) and 0.881 (functional) but abstained on 81.7\% of VUS.

\subsection{Certified likelihood ratios}\label{sec:evidence}
Treating each gene's assayed residues as the population (the oracle design), we drew calibration (30\%) and certification (50\%) residues
with replacement, 100 times per pair, ran \cref{alg:certivar} with $\delta=0.05$ and
$G_4=\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30\}$, and
computed the true residue-weighted likelihood ratio of every selection on the whole population. We compared
Clopper--Pearson bounds without multiplicity correction, a one-sided 95\% percentile bootstrap (2,000 resamples), the
same bootstrap at level $\delta/(2|G|)$, and the point estimate, all applied to the same data. Every bound claimed in a
draw was respected in 99.8\% of the 1,500 draws for the certified rule (Theorem~\ref{thm:lr} guarantees at least 90\%),
98.8\% for uncorrected bounds, 56.5\% for bootstrap bounds and 65.9\% for the corrected bootstrap
(\cref{fig:evidence}B); per selection, the bootstrap bound was respected in only 76.5\%. Most bootstrap failures came
from certification samples with no false call of that type, where the bound failed 89\% of the time. Claimed levels held
in all 2,181 certified claims, but in only 91.0\% of bootstrap and 79.7\% of point-estimate claims (\cref{fig:evidence}A).
On this grid the exact bound on one variant per residue, not the multiplicity correction, separates valid rules from
the others; the correction costs yield (LoF-moderate certified in 13.1\% of draws with it, 34.5\% without).

\textsc{CertiVar} certified LoF-supporting calls in 86.6\% of draws (calling 25.8\% of variants), functional-supporting
calls in 43.6\% (61.1\% called), LoF-moderate in 13.1\% and functional-moderate in 2.1\%, and never a strong level. In a fresh set
of 1,500 draws, per-class coverage at the selected $\alpha$ averaged 0.82 (LoF) and 0.80 (functional), above the
guaranteed 0.70 (Proposition~\ref{prop:select}): selecting for evidence strength trades coverage for confidence. Fixed-sequence testing, which certifies one level at a time, raised LoF-moderate certification in these draws from
12.9\% (Bonferroni) to 29.1\% of draws and functional-supporting from 42.5\% to 60.6\% (\cref{fig:shift}B), with 99.7\% of 2,705 claims holding; on a 20-level grid it kept LoF-moderate at 26.7\% where
the Bonferroni rule fell to 6.2\%. The testing order matters: functional-supporting, tested in increasing $\alpha$, was certified in only 25.7\% of draws (60.6\% in decreasing order). On held-out fifths of the residues, empirical likelihood ratios respected the
certified bounds in 98.7\% of 2,091 cases (bootstrap: 76.8\%); on VUS at other residues, LoF-supporting held in all 443 certified claims and LoF-moderate in 25 of 28. Against ClinVar labels (exploratory, not independent of the assays),
LoF calls had likelihood-ratio lower bounds of 4.9--19.6 (BRCA1), 7.6--9.4 (BRCA2) and 2.6--3.2 (MSH2) (Table~S4).

\subsection{How much data, from where, and with which labels}\label{sec:transfer}
Calibration data had to come from the same gene: calibrating on the other four genes left 8 of 15 pairs below 0.85
(worst 0.487), consistent with gene-specific evidence strength \cite{Tejura2024,Chen2026}. Calibrating on three
contiguous quarters of a protein and testing on the fourth gave worst-class coverage of 0.860--0.923, and the true ratio respected 88.8\% of 1,891 certified bounds, below the nominal 95\%. With 150 calibration residues drawn at random, coverage
averaged over genes met the target with 40.5\% abstention (82.8\% with 25 residues; Fig.~S2). Coverage was lower on the third of
variants nearest the assay class boundary (0.865 LoF, 0.873 functional). Intermediate variants, which no guarantee
covers, were called LoF in 19.6\% and functional in 36.6\% of cases; on 1,534 unassayed BRCA1 VUS outside the assayed
region, AlphaMissense thresholds abstained on only 53, which suggests a shifted score distribution. Relabelling BRCA2,
BAP1 and RAD51C with alternative rules (Table~S3) changed yields but not validity: worst-class coverage stayed between 0.894 and 0.908 over rules and predictors, and certified bounds held in 2,530 of 2,533 oracle selections.
