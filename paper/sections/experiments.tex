\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data and protocol}\label{sec:data}
\input{tables/tab_data}
We took every GRCh38 missense single-nucleotide variant in BRCA1, BRCA2, BAP1, RAD51C and MSH2 that is in ClinVar
\cite{Landrum2018} (release of 26 October 2026) or was assayed, annotated each on its MANE Select transcript with the
Ensembl Variant Effect Predictor \cite{McLaren2016} (AlphaMissense \cite{Cheng2023}, REVEL \cite{Ioannidis2016}) and
computed ESM-2 650M \cite{Lin2023} log-likelihood ratios by wild-type marginals \cite{Meier2021}. Labels come from five
MaveDB \cite{Esposito2019} score sets (\cref{tab:data}; accessions in \cref{s:impl}): the authors' classes for BRCA1,
BAP1 and RAD51C and the authors' threshold for MSH2 \cite{Jia2021}. The BRCA2 score set has no deposited classes, so we
fitted Gaussians to its synonymous and nonsense variants (LoF if the posterior is at least 0.95, functional if at most
0.05); applied where the authors' classes exist, this rule disagreed with them on 216 of 4,023 BAP1 and none of 1,762
BRCA1 variants (\cref{sec:transfer} gives other label rules). We excluded 729 splice-region and 1,274 intermediate
variants. One Platt map per predictor used the 859 ClinVar missense variants with at least two review stars and a
pathogenic or likely pathogenic (P/LP) or benign or likely benign (B/LB) call (45.1\% P/LP), which are excluded from
every evaluation set except the exploratory ClinVar check, leaving 14,477 assayed variants at 2,927 residues. AlphaMissense has an AUROC of 0.946
(95\% residue-bootstrap interval 0.922--0.965) on the ClinVar set, 0.840 (0.829--0.852) on the other assayed variants
and 0.779 (0.757--0.799) on assayed VUS. Baselines are pooled (marginal) split conformal prediction, label-shift-weighted
conformal prediction \cite{Podkopaev2021} with the true target share or its expectation--maximization (EM) estimate
\cite{Saerens2002}, fixed tiers ($q\ge0.9$ LoF, $q\le0.1$ functional), the published AlphaMissense classes ($>0.564$,
$<0.34$) and the REVEL PP3/BP4 thresholds ($\ge0.644$, $\le0.290$) \cite{Pejaver2022}; the published cut-offs target
pathogenicity, so we judge them only as assay classifiers. Coverage is residue-weighted as in $Q$; \emph{worst-class
coverage} is the smaller of the mean LoF and mean functional coverages over repetitions. Every split is by residue
(designs in \cref{tab:designs}).

\subsection{Per-class coverage and a shift in the calibration LoF share}\label{sec:coverage}
\input{tables/tab_methods}
Over 50 random halvings of each gene's residues, the in-expectation rule at $\alpha=0.1$ gave worst-class coverage
between 0.896 and 0.908 in all 15 gene--predictor pairs, none more than one Monte Carlo standard error (0.002--0.010)
below the target (\cref{tab:methods}; per pair in \cref{tab:main}). Pooled conformal fell to 0.545 on LoF variants and
below 0.85 in 5 pairs, because a marginal threshold controls coverage averaged over the two classes, not within each;
the published cut-offs covered functional variants as little as 0.540 (AlphaMissense, BAP1) and 0.445 (REVEL, MSH2).
The per-class rule abstained on 35\% of variants on average (17--47\%), against 42\% for pooled conformal and 11\% for
the AlphaMissense classes. Calibrating on all variants instead of one per residue lost coverage through its variant
weighting (\cref{s:figs}).

To compare the methods under a prevalence shift, we subsampled each calibration half to a LoF share of 0.05 to 0.5 and
tested at the natural share (100 repetitions; \cref{fig:shift}A). Subsampling by class leaves each class's score law
unchanged, so Corollary~\ref{cor:shift} predicts the per-class result: worst-class coverage stayed between 0.897 and
0.925 in every pair at every share, with abstention rising from 37\% to 83\% as LoF variants became scarce. Pooled
conformal calibrated at share 0.05 covered LoF variants at 0.113 in its worst pair, and label-shift-weighted conformal
fell to 0.53--0.60 with the true target share and to 0.04 with the EM estimate, which missed the LoF share by 0.255 on
average because ClinVar pathogenicity and assay loss of function differ in definition as well as in class share.

\subsection{Reliability across splits and a real change of population}\label{sec:shift}
With 17 to 93 LoF residues per calibration half, a single split can miss the in-expectation guarantee: LoF coverage on a
test half was below 0.90 in 36.4\% of 200 splits per pair and functional coverage in 43.6\% (\cref{fig:main}B). PAC
thresholds ($\delta_{\mathrm P}=0.1$) reduced these to 7.9\% and 10.8\% at the price of abstaining on 58.4\% of variants
against 35.4\%; computed exactly on all of a gene's residues, which removes the test half's sampling noise, PAC coverage
fell below 0.90 in 4.8\% (LoF) and 7.1\% (functional) of draws and in at most 10.0\% in any pair, within
$\delta_{\mathrm P}$. Calibrated on non-VUS variants of one half of each gene's residues and applied to the assayed VUS of
the other half (residue-weighted LoF share 17.0\% in calibration, 19.8\% in VUS), in-expectation thresholds kept LoF coverage between 0.854
and 0.958 and functional coverage between 0.841 and 0.942, each below 0.90 in 7 of 15 pairs (\cref{fig:main}C); PAC
thresholds abstained on 81.7\% of VUS and still fell below 0.90 for functional variants in 2 pairs (lowest 0.881). On
VUS, Assumption~\ref{as:shift} holds only approximately.

\subsection{Certified likelihood ratios}\label{sec:evidence}
\input{tables/tab_oracle}
Treating each gene's assayed residues as the population, we drew calibration (30\%) and certification (50\%) residues
with replacement, 100 times per pair, ran \cref{alg:certivar} with $\delta=0.05$ on the four-point grid
$G_4=\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30\}$, and computed the population likelihood ratio of every selection exactly, applying all
rules to the same draws (\cref{tab:oracle}); Assumption~\ref{as:cluster} holds here by construction, and
\cref{sec:transfer} tests held-out regions. Every
bound claimed in a draw held in 99.7\% of the 1,500 draws for the certified rule (Theorem~\ref{thm:lr} guarantees at
least 90\%). Percentile-bootstrap bounds all held in only 57.6\% of draws, 65.3\% with a Bonferroni correction and 75.4\%
with 0.5 added to every count; 59\% of their failures came from certification samples with no false call of that type.
Point-estimate claims all held in only 42.1\% of draws (\cref{tab:oracle,fig:evidence}). Uncorrected Clopper--Pearson bounds and
Katz intervals with a Bonferroni correction, which have no guarantee after selection, held in 97.9\% and 98.0\% of draws
and certified more often: Katz bounds reached LoF-moderate in 52.5\% of draws against 13.3\%.

\textsc{CertiVar} certified LoF-supporting calls in 86.7\% of draws (calling 25.2\% of variants), functional-supporting
calls in 44.9\% (61.4\% called), LoF-moderate in 13.3\% and functional-moderate in 1.5\%, and never a strong level.
Per-class coverage at the selected $\alpha$ averaged 0.81 (LoF) and 0.80 (functional), above the guaranteed 0.70
(Proposition~\ref{prop:select}). Fixed-sequence testing with the level-simultaneous guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:fs}(b)
traded supporting for moderate evidence: on $G_4$ it certified LoF-moderate in 14.4\% of draws (Bonferroni: 13.3\%) but
LoF-supporting in 74.9\% (86.7\%), and on a 20-point grid of values from 0.02 to 0.40, LoF-moderate in 12.7\% (6.4\%)
but LoF-supporting in 50.3\% (74.8\%; \cref{fig:shift}B). On held-out fifths of the residues, empirical likelihood ratios
respected the certified bounds in 98.7\% of 2,091 cases (bootstrap: 76.8\%); on VUS at other residues, LoF-supporting
held in all 443 certified claims and LoF-moderate in 25 of 28. An exploratory check against ClinVar labels is in
\cref{s:figs}.

\subsection{Checking invariance, and where CertiVar applies}\label{sec:check}
\input{tables/tab_mixture}
The mixture-consistency check rejected 4.7\% of random halves (3.0--7.7\% by gene), where Assumption~\ref{as:shift}
holds by design (\cref{tab:mixture}). It rejected 43.9\% of held-out contiguous quarters, 77.3\% of transfers from
another gene and every set of unassayed ClinVar VUS at residues the BRCA1 and BRCA2 assays did not reach, where the
thresholds abstained on only 8.9\% of variants against 35\% on assayed residues. Rejected targets had lower coverage than
accepted ones, but the check has little power for assayed VUS (8.5\% rejected), so passing it is not evidence that the
guarantees transfer. Of the 147 ClinVar VUS that were unassayed but lay at assayed residues, 105 are in MSH2; there the
check rejected 40\% of samples with AlphaMissense and 35\% with REVEL but none with ESM-2, whose thresholds gave
certified LoF-supporting calls to 19.1 and functional-supporting calls to 5.0 of the 105 on average over 20 splits
(\cref{tab:app_usecase,tab:app_usecase_calls}). The 4,010 unassayed BRCA1 and BRCA2 VUS at other residues are not called,
because the check rejected every sample of them.

\subsection{How much data, from where, and with which labels}\label{sec:transfer}
Calibration data had to come from the same gene: calibrating on the other four genes left 8 of 15 pairs below 0.85
(worst 0.487), consistent with gene-specific evidence strength \cite{Tejura2024,Chen2026}. Calibrating on three contiguous
quarters of a protein and testing on the fourth gave worst-class coverage of 0.860--0.923, and 88.8\% of 1,891 certified
bounds held against the held-out quarter's empirical likelihood ratios, below the 95\% that Theorem~\ref{thm:lr}
guarantees under Assumption~\ref{as:cluster}. Intermediate variants, which no guarantee covers, were called LoF in
19.6\% and functional in 36.6\% of cases. Relabelling BRCA2, BAP1 and RAD51C with alternative rules
(\cref{tab:app_labels}) changed yields but not validity: the lowest per-class coverage over predictors stayed between
0.894 and 0.902, and certified bounds held in 2,530 of 2,533 oracle selections. Calibration size and class-boundary
strata are in \cref{s:practical,s:strata}.
