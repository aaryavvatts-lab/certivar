\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data and protocol}\label{sec:data}
\input{tables/tab_data}
We took every GRCh38 single-nucleotide missense variant (SNV) in BRCA1, BRCA2, BAP1, RAD51C and MSH2 that is in
ClinVar \cite{Landrum2018} (release of 26 October 2026) or was assayed, annotated each on its Matched Annotation from
NCBI and EMBL-EBI (MANE) Select transcript with the Ensembl Variant Effect Predictor \cite{McLaren2016}
(AlphaMissense \cite{Cheng2023}, REVEL \cite{Ioannidis2016}) and computed ESM-2 650M \cite{Lin2023} log-likelihood
ratios by wild-type marginals \cite{Meier2021}. Labels come from five MaveDB \cite{Esposito2019} score sets
(\cref{tab:data}; accessions and versions in Supplement~S5). For BRCA1, BAP1 and RAD51C we used the assay authors'
classes (for BAP1 recomputed from the deposited false-discovery rates and score signs, which reproduces the deposited
class counts exactly), and for MSH2 the authors' threshold (a positive loss-of-function score \cite{Jia2021}). BRCA2
deposits no per-variant classes in MaveDB, so its labels come from Gaussians fitted to the assay's own synonymous and
nonsense variants (LoF if the posterior is at least 0.95, functional if at most 0.05). Compared with the authors'
classes, this control rule made no opposite call in BRCA1 or RAD51C, but it gave the opposite call for 216 of the 4,023
BAP1 variants that both rules label and left 77\% of the authors' functional RAD51C variants intermediate, so we repeat the
main experiments under alternative label rules (\cref{sec:transfer}). We excluded the 729 assayed missense variants that
also touch a splice region, since SGE also measures splicing, and set aside the 1,274 intermediate variants
(\cref{sec:transfer}). No ClinVar label enters an assay label.

One Platt map per predictor, pooled over genes, used the 859 ClinVar missense variants with at least two review stars
and a P/LP or B/LB call (45.1\% P/LP); these are excluded from every assay-label evaluation set, which leaves 14,477
assayed variants with a binary label. Of the 859, 422 also have a binary assay label, and for these P/LP matched LoF and
B/LB matched functional for 97.4\%, partly because some ClinVar classifications already use these assays. Pooled over
genes, AlphaMissense has an area under the ROC curve (AUROC) of 0.946 on the ClinVar set, 0.840 on the other assayed
variants and 0.779 on assayed VUS (non-overlapping 95\% residue-bootstrap intervals), a drop seen for other predictors
in DMS benchmarks \cite{Livesey2023}. Baselines are pooled (marginal) split conformal prediction on the same
calibration data; label-shift-weighted conformal prediction \cite{Podkopaev2021} with the true target share or its
expectation--maximization (EM) estimate \cite{Saerens2002}; fixed tiers ($q\ge0.9$ LoF, $q\le0.1$ functional); the published AlphaMissense classes (likely pathogenic
$>0.564$, likely benign $<0.34$); and the REVEL PP3/BP4 supporting thresholds ($\ge0.644$, $\le0.290$)
\cite{Pejaver2022}. None claims per-class coverage, and the published cut-offs target pathogenicity, so we judge them only as
assay classifiers. Coverage is
residue-weighted as in $Q$; \emph{worst-class coverage} is the smaller of the mean LoF and mean functional coverages over
repetitions. \Cref{tab:designs} lists the designs.

\subsection{Per-class coverage, and a shift in the calibration LoF share}\label{sec:coverage}\label{sec:priorres}
In each gene we split the residues in half 50 times, calibrated on one half and evaluated on the other. At $\alpha=0.1$
the in-expectation rule gave worst-class coverage between 0.896 and 0.908 in all 15 gene--predictor pairs, none more than
one Monte Carlo standard error (0.002--0.010) below the target (\cref{tab:main}, \cref{fig:main}A); it was at least 0.948 at
$\alpha=0.05$ and at least 0.792 at $\alpha=0.2$. Pooled conformal fell to 0.545 on LoF variants in its worst pair and
below 0.85 in 5 pairs, because a marginal threshold spends its error budget on the rarer class; where it covered LoF
variants, as for AlphaMissense in BAP1, it abstained on 69\% of variants. The fixed tiers fell below 0.85 in 5 pairs, and
the published AlphaMissense and REVEL cut-offs covered functional variants as little as 0.540 (BAP1) and 0.445 (MSH2).
The per-class rule abstained on 35\% of variants on average (17--47\%), against 42\% for pooled conformal, 53\% for the
fixed tiers, 42\% for the REVEL cut-offs and 11\% for the AlphaMissense classes, which trade coverage for decisiveness.
In a separate set of 50 splits, calibrating on all variants of the calibration half instead of one per residue used
five times as many labels (1,446
against 292 on average) and lowered abstention from 35.2\% to 30.4\%, but worst-class coverage fell below 0.90 in 13 of
15 pairs (0.887--0.903) against 4 of 15 (0.897--0.915), as expected when substitutions at one residue are dependent.

To test the claim that per-class thresholds need no prevalence estimate, we subsampled each calibration half (one
variant per residue, 36 to 176 variants depending on the gene) to a LoF share of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 or 0.5 and applied
the thresholds to the other half at its natural share (100 repetitions; \cref{fig:shift}A). The per-class rule kept
worst-class coverage between 0.897 and 0.925 in every pair at every share (0.891--0.925 in a separate run that balanced
the whole calibration half to 50\% LoF), at the cost of abstention that rose from
37\% at share 0.5 to 83\% at share 0.05, when few LoF variants remain to set the LoF threshold. Pooled conformal
calibrated at share 0.05 covered LoF variants at 0.113 in its worst pair, and label-shift-weighted conformal fell to
0.53--0.60 in its worst pair with the true target share and to 0.04 with the EM estimate. The estimate is the weak point:
from unlabelled ClinVar-calibrated scores, EM missed the LoF share of a gene's assayed variants by
0.255 on average and by up to 0.702 (black-box shift estimation \cite{Lipton2018}: 0.233; Supplement~S3), because
ClinVar pathogenicity and assay loss of function differ in label definition as well as in class share.

\subsection{Reliability across splits and a real change of population}\label{sec:shift}
With 17 to 93 LoF residues per calibration half, a single split can fall short of the in-expectation guarantee: over 200
splits per pair, LoF coverage was below 0.90 in 36.4\% of splits and functional coverage in 43.6\% (\cref{fig:main}B).
PAC thresholds ($\delta=0.1$) reduced these to 7.9\% and 10.8\% (the latter above $\delta$ because coverage on a finite
test half is a noisy estimate of the true coverage) at the price of abstaining on 58.4\% of variants against 35.4\%. To
test a real change of population, we set thresholds on non-VUS variants of one half of each gene's residues and called
the assayed VUS of the other half (mean residue-weighted LoF share 17.0\% in calibration and 19.8\% among VUS; 50
repetitions). In-expectation thresholds kept LoF coverage between 0.854 and 0.958 and functional coverage between 0.841
and 0.942, each below 0.90 in 7 of 15 pairs (\cref{fig:main}C). PAC thresholds gave LoF coverage between 0.957 and 1.000
and functional coverage between 0.881 and 0.965, below 0.90 in 2 pairs, but abstained on 81.7\% of VUS against 46.4\%,
because the PAC rank is conservative when each class has few calibration residues.

\subsection{Certified likelihood ratios}\label{sec:evidence}
Treating each gene's assayed residues as the population, we drew calibration (30\% of residues) and certification
(50\%) samples with replacement, 100 times per pair, ran \cref{alg:certivar} with $G_4=\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30\}$,
$\delta=0.05$ and the in-expectation rank for each level, and computed the true residue-weighted likelihood ratio of every
selected rule on the whole population. We compared four other rules applied to the same data: Clopper--Pearson bounds
without multiplicity correction, a one-sided 95\% percentile bootstrap of the one-variant-per-residue certification
sample (2,000 resamples), the same bootstrap at level $\delta/(2|G|)$, and the point estimate. Calibration workflows use
the bootstrap for a local likelihood ratio \cite{Pejaver2022}, so this compares uncertainty estimates, not published
thresholds. A bound is respected when the true $\mathrm{LR}^+$ is at least $L^+$ (LoF calls) or the true $\mathrm{LR}^-$
is at most $U^-$ (functional calls).

Every bound claimed in a draw was respected in 99.8\% of the 1,500 draws for the certified rule (Theorem~\ref{thm:lr}
guarantees at least 90\%), 98.8\% for uncorrected Clopper--Pearson bounds, 56.5\% for bootstrap bounds and 65.9\% for the
corrected bootstrap (\cref{fig:evidence}B). Per selection, the certified bound was respected in 2,178 of 2,181 cases
(99.9\%), uncorrected bounds in 99.2\% of 3,115 and bootstrap bounds in 76.5\% of 5,287. Most bootstrap failures are
degenerate resamples: in a fresh set of 1,500 draws, 57\% of failed bootstrap bounds came from certification samples
with no false call of that type, where the bound failed 89\% of the time, but bounds failed in 11\% of cases even with
nonzero counts. A point estimate is not a bound; judged by whether the claimed level truly held, the certified rule held
in all 2,181 claims, bootstrap bounds in 91.0\% and point estimates in 79.7\%: point estimates claimed LoF-moderate in
98.6\% of draws and it held in 81.1\% of claims (\cref{fig:evidence}A). The true ratio weighted by variants instead of
residues gave the same conclusions (levels held in 99.9\% of certified claims). On the four-level grid, the exact binomial bound
on one variant per residue, not the multiplicity correction, separates the valid rules from the others; the correction
costs yield (LoF-moderate certified in 13.1\% of draws with it, 34.5\% without).

\textsc{CertiVar} certified LoF-supporting calls in 86.6\% of draws (74--97\% by gene; 25.8\% of variants called),
functional-supporting calls in 43.6\% (61.1\% called), LoF-moderate in 13.1\% and functional-moderate in 2.1\%, and never
a strong level. In a fresh set of 1,500 draws, at the selected $\alpha$ (mean 0.18 for LoF and 0.24 for functional calls),
per-class coverage on the population averaged 0.82 for LoF and 0.80 for functional variants, above the 0.70 that Proposition~\ref{prop:select}
guarantees for $\max G_4=0.3$, but it fell below 0.69 in a tenth of selections: selecting for evidence strength trades
coverage for confidence in the calls that are made. On the same 1,500 fresh draws, fixed-sequence testing
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fs}) raised the share of draws certifying LoF-moderate from 12.9\% to 29.1\% and functional-supporting
from 42.5\% to 60.6\%, with claimed levels holding in 99.7\% of 2,705 claims and no level falsely claimed in more than
0.4\% of draws (\cref{fig:shift}B). On a 20-level grid $G_{20}$ ($\alpha$ from 0.02 to 0.40) the Bonferroni level
shrinks and LoF-moderate fell to 6.2\%, whereas fixed-sequence testing kept 26.7\%; it lost LoF-supporting yield (65.3\%
against 73.8\%) because its first, smallest $\alpha$ calls few variants. Testing functional levels in increasing
$\alpha$ instead certified functional-supporting in only 25.7\% ($G_4$) and 0.2\% ($G_{20}$) of draws, so the order
matters and must be fixed in advance.

In data splits that held out a test fifth of the residues (100 per pair), the empirical residue-weighted likelihood
ratio on the test fifth respected the certified bounds in 98.7\% of 2,091 cases, against 76.8\% for bootstrap bounds.
Calibrated and certified on non-VUS variants and applied to VUS at other residues, the LoF-supporting level was
certified in 59.1\% of repetitions and the empirical likelihood ratio on the VUS met it in all 443; the
functional-supporting level held in 123 of 125 and the LoF-moderate level in 25 of 28 (89\%; 95\% interval 72--98\%).

The certificates concern the assay outcome. As an exploratory check against clinical labels, we set per-class
thresholds ($\alpha=0.1$) on all assay-labelled residues of each gene and called one ClinVar variant per residue among
the 859 with at least two stars, which never enter the thresholds; likelihood-ratio bounds against P/LP versus B/LB use
one-sided Clopper--Pearson endpoints at level 0.025 each (Table~S4). In the three genes with enough labelled residues
(BRCA1: 75 P/LP and 170 B/LB; BRCA2: 60 and 210; MSH2: 72 and 55), LoF calls had lower bounds of 4.9--19.6 in BRCA1,
7.6--9.4 in BRCA2 and 2.6--3.2 in MSH2 across predictors, and functional calls upper bounds of 0.24--0.37. Confidently
classified variants are easier than typical VUS, and some ClinVar classifications already use these assays or
predictors as evidence, so this is not independent clinical validation.

\subsection{How much data, from where, and with which labels}\label{sec:transfer}
In our five genes, calibration data had to come from the gene itself: calibrating on the other four genes left 8 of 15
pairs below 0.85, with a worst value of 0.487 (Fig.~S2C), consistent with gene-specific evidence strength
\cite{Tejura2024,Chen2026}. Calibrating on three contiguous quarters of a protein and testing on the fourth gave
worst-class coverage between 0.860 and 0.923; certified bounds were respected in the held-out quarter in 88.8\% of 1,891
cases and the claimed level held in 93.0\%, below the nominal 95\%, because another region's residues are not
exchangeable with the calibrated ones. With 150 calibration residues drawn at random, coverage averaged over genes and
predictors met the target with 40.5\% abstention, against 39.8\% with 200 and 82.8\% with 25 (Fig.~S2A). Coverage is
marginal within a class: on the third of variants whose assay score lies nearest the boundary between the two class
medians it was 0.865 for LoF and 0.873 for functional variants (one split per pair, variant-weighted; Supplement~S4). The
1,262 intermediate variants outside the ClinVar labelled set (BRCA2 888, BAP1 210, BRCA1 155, RAD51C 9), which no
guarantee covers, were called LoF in 19.6\% and functional in 36.6\% of cases. Applied outside the assayed region, to 1,534 unassayed BRCA1 VUS, AlphaMissense thresholds called
1,463 functional and abstained on only 53, far fewer abstentions than on assayed variants; this suggests a shifted
score distribution there, which no guarantee covers. Finally, we relabelled BRCA2 with control-rule cut-offs of 0.80 and 0.99, and
BAP1 and RAD51C with the control rule instead of the authors' classes (Table~S3, an independent re-run with its own
random splits). The label rule changed the yield: under the control rule 10\% of labelled BAP1 variants were LoF rather
than 21\%, and abstention fell from 39--47\% to 13--19\%. It did not change validity: under the four alternative rules,
worst-class coverage stayed between 0.894 and 0.908 across gene--predictor pairs, and certified bounds held in 2,530 of
2,533 oracle selections.
