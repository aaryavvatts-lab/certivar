\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

In our resampling design, thresholds selected on point estimates or percentile-bootstrap bounds often claimed more
evidence than held, and most bootstrap failures came from certification samples with no false call. Because these are
call-averaged ratios, not the local ratios of ClinGen calibrations, this does not show that published PP3/BP4
thresholds are wrong, only that point estimates and bootstrap bounds can overstate evidence at single-gene sample sizes.
\textsc{CertiVar} makes the claim checkable without an estimate of the share of damaging variants, and per-class
coverage stayed at the target when the calibration LoF share was moved from 5\% to 50\%. The cost is conservatism:
certified evidence was mostly supporting-level, and PAC thresholds abstained on 58\% to 82\% of variants, suited to
settings where overstating strength is worse than abstaining. Fixed-sequence testing recovers part of the yield that a
multiplicity correction loses, provided the order is fixed before the data are seen. Held-out protein regions, a proxy
for the unassayed regions where predictors are most needed, fall outside the theory: coverage there ranged from 0.860
to 0.923 and certified bounds held in 88.8\% of cases rather than 95\%, so extrapolation needs local validation or a
region-aware procedure.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Assay, not pathogenicity.} The labels are assay classes \cite{Brnich2020}; clinical use of the certified
ratios requires an assay validated against clinical classifications, as its authors did for BRCA1 \cite{Findlay2018}
and, with their own classes rather than our control-based labels, for BRCA2 \cite{Huang2025}.
\item \emph{Call-averaged, not local.} A certified level describes the calls as a class. Variants near the assay class
boundary are covered less often (0.865 and 0.873 on the nearest third), so a single borderline call can carry less
evidence than the level of its class; ClinGen uses local ratios for this reason \cite{Pejaver2022}.
\item \emph{Who is covered.} The guarantees assume residues exchangeable with the calibrated ones and do not cover
intermediate or splice-region variants, which a clinician cannot exclude in advance; 19.6\% of intermediate variants were
called LoF. Per-class coverage holds for a fixed $\alpha$; at the selected $\alpha$ only $1-\max G$ is guaranteed.
\item \emph{Data.} One variant per residue discards most labels, but using all of them broke coverage in our data.
Results cover five cancer-predisposition genes measured by SGE or DMS, four of them in DNA repair; other genes and
assays may need more calibration residues. REVEL was trained on labelled variants and the AlphaMissense classes were set
on ClinVar, so both may overlap our evaluation sets \cite{Ioannidis2016,Cheng2023,Grimm2015}. ESM-2, trained without
variant labels, gave the same coverage and validity but certified LoF-supporting calls less often (78\% of oracle draws,
against 86\% for REVEL and 96\% for AlphaMissense), so such overlap could inflate yields rather than validity.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\textsc{CertiVar} turns a variant-effect predictor and a few hundred assayed residues of a gene into LoF, functional or
no calls with finite-sample guarantees on per-class error rates at a fixed miscoverage level and on call-averaged
likelihood ratios with respect to the assay after threshold selection, without an estimate of how common damaging
variants are. The guarantees are proved under residue sampling and class-conditional invariance; they were consistent
with the data in random within-gene splits and under shifts of the calibration LoF share, held approximately on assayed
VUS, and weakened in held-out protein regions. The calls are research results; using the certificates as clinical
evidence requires clinically validated assays.
